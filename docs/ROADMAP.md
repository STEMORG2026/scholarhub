## Current development focus (NOW)

- **Reading the reader's own documents — Phase 1 done, the requirement schema done, the sourcing pass is a decision.** [`DOCUMENT-INGESTION.md`](DOCUMENT-INGESTION.md) proposed the pipeline; **v0.28.0 built the half that was unblocked.** A reader can now attach a `.docx` or a text file and ScholarHub reads the figures out of it **on the device**, showing each one beside the text it came from so it can be corrected. The comparison engine is written and returns `meets` / `fails` / **`unknown`** — and it currently answers `unknown` for **45 of 50 records**, because the catalog does not carry requirements as data. **v0.29.0 removed the last excuse for that**: [`REQUIREMENTS-SCHEMA.md`](REQUIREMENTS-SCHEMA.md) defines the requirement entity and [`data/requirements.json`](data/requirements.json) proves it against the **five hardest records** — the five whose requirement is prose, all transcribed verbatim. The shape can hold a figure on four different GPA scales (`branches`), a threshold split by field, and an explicit **"the provider states there is no threshold"** that is *not* the same fact as "we have not looked". Every requirement carries `source` and `last_verified`, and `scripts/validate-requirements.mjs` rejects twelve defect classes — each one verified to fire. **What remains is a sourcing pass across the other 45 records: writing data into a schema that already exists and is already enforced, not designing anything.** That is the owner's decision, and it is now the only thing between this and a comparison that answers `meets` or `fails`. A PDF reader (~1.4 MB, about three times the bundle) and OCR remain deliberately deferred.

- **Data expansion**: 50 records now, and **broader Europe has reached the Phase 1 target of 15–25 at its lower bound (15)** — the first region to do so. China holds 5, New Zealand and the United States 4 each, and every other Phase 1 destination holds 2. **Phase 2 is complete**: **Canada** and **Japan** opened four passes ago, **South Korea** and **India** after them, then **Saudi Arabia**, **Brazil**, and now **Africa** with the **Pan African University Scholarship** — so the Phase 2 list is exhausted and the next region is **South America beyond Brazil**. Four records are worth singling out. India is the first here **reserved for Nepal** rather than merely open to it — 64 places no other nationality can use. KAUST is the first with **no quota and no nationality restriction at all**. **PEC-PG is the first whose headline fact is a restriction**: 74 named countries, and Nepal is not among them. And **PAU is the first recorded under a regional rule that cannot be resolved to a list**: "African countries and the African Diaspora" reports *check* for every reader, including readers in Africa, because a provider's "Africa" is not a continent and the Diaspora is not a country list. **That rule is the exception, not the pattern** — three of the four `regional` records turned out to have a published list after all, and are now `listed` (Australia Awards 59, Manaaki NZ Thematic 68, and the NL Scholarship's "non-EEA" rule carried as the 30 EEA members in the negative). A `regional` scope should now be treated as a **lead to chase**, not an answer: check whether the provider publishes the list before accepting "check". **USA growth is structurally slower** because most US graduate funding is restricted to citizens and permanent residents — the pass that took the USA from 2 to 4 deliberately targeted awards open to Nepali applicants. Breadth remains the largest gap: provenance is done, coverage is not.
- **Live application windows**: most records sit between cycles, so most carry `deadline_notes` instead of a `deadline`. **Eleven** now have a single verified closing date: **Knight-Hennessy Scholars, 6 October 2026** (now closed — the 2028 cycle opens in summer 2027), **ETH Zurich ESOP, 30 November 2026**, **TU Delft Justus & Louise van Effen, 1 December 2026**, the **KAUST Fellowship, 3 January 2027** (the open Fall 2027 round; the Spring 2027 round was PhD-only and closed on 27 September 2026), **Erasmus Mundus Flood Risk Management and Groundwater and Global Change, both 3 January 2027** (Partner Country applicants), **KTH Scholarship, 15 January 2027**, **EMerald Georesources, 28 February 2027**, the **Japanese Government (MEXT) Research Student scholarship, 31 May 2026**, the **Global Korea Scholarship for Graduate Degrees, 25 February 2026** (the 2026 Embassy Track deadline) and the **ICCR Sushma Swaraj Silver Jubilee Scholarship Scheme, 15 April 2026**. The last three dates are **mission-set rather than scheme-set** — Nepal's own deadlines, published by the Embassy of Japan in Kathmandu, NIIED's Embassy Track and the Embassy of India in Kathmandu respectively — and all three have passed. The next calls are expected around April 2027, February 2027 and late February 2027. One record sits deliberately outside this count: the **Canada Graduate Research Scholarship – Doctoral**, whose deadline NSERC publishes as "October 17" with no year at all, so `deadline` is `null` and the date is carried in `deadline_notes` instead. **EESIC's 2027-2029 call opened in October 2026** and its closing date is still unpublished; the previous call's reference date — **10 February 2026, 12:00 CET** — is now recorded in `deadline_notes` alongside the 2026-2028 call's credit requirements, so an applicant can prepare against a real rhythm without a fabricated 2027 date.

## Principles

1. **No authentication.** The app is fully public. Personalization stays in the browser.
2. **No paid services required.** AI features are optional enhancements using user-provided keys.
3. **Official sources are authoritative.** This catalog is a discovery aid, not a replacement for provider pages.
4. **Build small.** Verified increments over speculative infrastructure.
5. **AI output is not canonical.** Generated content requires human review before commit.
